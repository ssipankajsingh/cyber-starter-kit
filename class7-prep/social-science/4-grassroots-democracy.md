## The big idea

In a **democracy**, people choose who governs them. India has government at three levels: the **Union** (country), the **State**, and the **local** level, which is the one closest to people. "Grassroots" means the roots of the grass, right at the bottom. **Grassroots democracy** means giving ordinary people in villages and towns a say in running their own area.

In villages, local government is called the **Panchayati Raj** system. (*Panch* means five, and *panchayat* was traditionally a group of respected elders.)

## The three tiers (levels)

| Level | Name | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Village | **Gram Panchayat** | one village or a group of villages |
| 2. Block | **Panchayat Samiti** | a group of villages (a block) |
| 3. District | **Zila Parishad** | the whole district |

## Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat

- The **Gram Sabha** is the meeting of **all the adults** (voters) of a village. It's the most basic level of democracy. Everyone can speak, ask questions, and discuss village needs and the panchayat's accounts.
- The **Gram Panchayat** is the elected body that runs the village day to day.
- The village is divided into **wards**. Each ward elects a **ward member** (also called a **Panch**).
- The head of the Gram Panchayat is the **Sarpanch** (also called a **Pradhan** in some places), who is also elected.
- Everyone aged **18 or above** can vote.
- Members are elected for **5 years**.
- **Reservation of seats:** at least **one-third** of the seats are reserved for **women**, and seats are also reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. Some states have set aside even more seats for women.

## What does a Gram Panchayat do?

- Provides **drinking water**, repairs wells and hand pumps
- Builds and repairs **village roads** and drains
- Looks after **street lights**, cleanliness and **sanitation**
- Runs and looks after **schools**, health centres and anganwadis
- Keeps records of births and deaths
- Helps villagers with government schemes

**Where does the money come from?**
- Taxes it collects (such as house tax and market fees)
- **Grants** (money given) by the State and the Union government

**Other village officials:** the **Patwari** (or *Lekhpal*) keeps records of land. The **Gram Sevak** or panchayat secretary helps with paperwork.

## Why Panchayati Raj matters

- Decisions are made by people who know the village's problems best.
- Ordinary people, including women and weaker sections of society, get a chance to take part in governing.
- It makes the government **closer and more accountable**. If the Sarpanch does a poor job, the voters can choose someone else in the next election.
- It was given constitutional status by the **73rd Amendment** (passed in 1992, in force from 1993).

## Memory tips

- **G-P-Z: Gram, Panchayat Samiti, Zila.** Village, Block, District. Small to big.
- **"Gram Sabha = Group of All."** Every adult voter is a member.
- **Panch means 5:** and a Panchayat is a small elected council.
- **Women: one in three.** At least one-third of seats.
- **18 to vote, 5 to serve.** Voting age 18, term of 5 years.
- **Three tiers = three levels, bottom to top: Village, Block, District.**

## Mistakes to avoid

- Mixing up the Gram Sabha (all voters, a meeting) and the Gram Panchayat (the elected council).
- Swapping the order. The Gram Panchayat is the **lowest** tier, and the Zila Parishad is the **highest** of the three.
- Writing that only men can be Sarpanch. Women can, and many are.
- Saying the Panchayat gets all its money from the government. It collects its own taxes too.
