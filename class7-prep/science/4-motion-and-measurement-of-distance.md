## Measuring length

To **measure** something is to compare it with a **standard unit**. Why standard? Because a hand-span or a foot-length is different for every person. A "metre" is the same for everyone.

**Units of length**
- The SI unit (the international standard unit) of length is the **metre (m)**.
- 1 m = 100 cm. 1 cm = 10 mm. 1 km = 1000 m.

**Tools:** a ruler (cm and mm), a measuring tape (long distances), a tailor's tape (curved things).

**How to measure properly**
1. Place the ruler **right along** the object, with the zero mark at one end. If the end of the ruler is worn, start from the 1 cm mark, and subtract 1 cm at the end.
2. Put your **eye directly above** the mark you're reading. Looking from the side gives a wrong reading.
3. Read to the nearest mark.

**Measuring a curved line:** lay a thread along the curve, mark both ends, straighten the thread and measure it against the ruler.

**Distance** is how far apart two places are, measured along the path you travel.

## Types of motion

Something is in **motion** when its position keeps changing as time passes.

| Type | How it moves | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectilinear** (straight line) | along a straight path | a car on a straight road, a falling stone |
| **Circular** | in a circle | a stone whirled on a string, the tip of a clock's hand |
| **Rotational** | spinning around its own centre or axis | a top, a ceiling fan, the Earth turning on its axis |
| **Oscillatory** (to and fro) | back and forth around a middle point | a swing, a pendulum, a guitar string |

A motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time is called **periodic**. A pendulum and the Earth going round the Sun are periodic. A rolling wheel does two things at once: it spins (rotational) and it moves along the road (rectilinear).

## Speed

**Speed** tells you how fast something moves: how much distance it covers in one unit of time.

- **Speed = distance ÷ time**
- **Distance = speed × time**
- **Time = distance ÷ speed**

Units: **m/s** (metres per second) and **km/h** (kilometres per hour).

**Converting:** 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s. So:
- km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18. (72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.)
- m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5. (10 m/s = 10 × 18/5 = 36 km/h.)

**The magic triangle:** draw a triangle with *distance* on top and *speed* and *time* at the bottom. Cover the one you want: if distance is covered, it's speed × time. If speed is covered, it's distance ÷ time.

## Solved examples

**1. A car covers 120 km in 2 hours. What's its speed?**
Speed = 120 ÷ 2 = **60 km/h**.

**2. A runner covers 100 m in 20 s. Find the speed.**
100 ÷ 20 = **5 m/s**.

**3. A bus travels at 50 km/h for 3 hours. How far does it go?**
Distance = 50 × 3 = **150 km**.

**4. How long to travel 90 km at 45 km/h?**
Time = 90 ÷ 45 = **2 hours**.

**5. A train travels at 54 km/h. What's that in m/s?**
54 × 5/18 = **15 m/s**.

## Memory tips

- **Speed is "S = D over T".** Say "S-D-T". Distance on top, time at the bottom.
- **For km/h to m/s, "5 over 18".** *Five-eighteenths.* A handy shortcut: dividing by 3.6 does the same thing.
- **Four kinds of motion by picture:** a ruler sliding (straight), a stone on a string (circle), a top (spin), a swing (to and fro).
- **Measure with your eye level.** Imagine looking straight down at the mark like a hawk.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Using different units together. Don't divide kilometres by seconds and call it km/h. Convert first.
- Measuring a curved line with a stiff ruler. Use a thread.
- Starting from a worn end of the ruler without allowing for it.
- Reading a ruler from an angle.
- Mixing up rotational (spinning in place) and circular (travelling around a circle).

- Speed = distance ÷ time. Distance = speed × time. Time = distance ÷ speed.
