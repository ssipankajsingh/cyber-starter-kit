## Temperature and heat

**Temperature** tells you **how hot or cold** something is. We measure it with a thermometer, in degrees Celsius (°C).

**Heat** is a form of energy that moves from a hotter thing to a colder thing.

They're different. A small cup of boiling water has a higher temperature than a bucket of warm water, but the bucket holds more heat, because there's much more of it.

**Your hand can fool you.** Put one hand in cold water and one in hot water, then both into lukewarm water. One hand says "warm", the other says "cool". So we use a thermometer, not our fingers.

## The thermometer

A thermometer is a thin glass tube with a bulb at the bottom, filled with mercury (a silver liquid metal) or red-dyed alcohol. When the bulb gets hot, the liquid **expands** and climbs up the tube. The scale beside it shows the temperature.

**Two kinds you must know**

| | Laboratory thermometer | Clinical thermometer |
|---|---|---|
| Used for | water and other liquids, experiments | our body temperature |
| Range | −10 °C to 110 °C | 35 °C to 42 °C |
| Special feature | none | a **kink** (a narrow bend) near the bulb |
| Reading | read while it's in the liquid | can be read after removing it from the body |

**Why the kink?** It stops the mercury from sliding back down by itself when you take the thermometer out of your mouth. That keeps the reading in place so you can read it.

**Normal body temperature** is **37 °C** (98.6 °F).

**Using a clinical thermometer safely**
1. Wash it with antiseptic, and shake it so the mercury is below 35 °C.
2. Put the bulb under the tongue or under the armpit for about a minute.
3. Take it out and read it, keeping your eye level with the mercury.
4. Wash it again.

**Never** use a clinical thermometer to measure hot water or the room. It only goes up to 42 °C and would break.

**Using a laboratory thermometer:** keep it upright, don't let the bulb touch the bottom or the sides of the container, and read it with your eye level with the mercury while the bulb is still in the liquid.

(Mercury is poisonous. If a thermometer breaks, don't touch the silver beads. Tell an adult. Many places now use digital thermometers instead.)

## Temperature scales

| | Celsius | Fahrenheit | Kelvin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water freezes | 0 °C | 32 °F | 273 K |
| Water boils | 100 °C | 212 °F | 373 K |

**Converting**
- °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
- °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
- K = °C + 273

Example: 37 °C → 37 × 9/5 + 32 = 66.6 + 32 = **98.6 °F**.
Example: 86 °F → (86 − 32) × 5/9 = 54 × 5/9 = **30 °C**.

## How heat travels

Heat always flows from the **hotter** object to the **colder** one, until they're at the same temperature.

| Way | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | through a solid, from particle to particle | a metal spoon gets hot in hot tea |
| **Convection** | through liquids and gases, as the hot part rises and the cool part sinks | water boiling in a pan, sea breeze |
| **Radiation** | as rays, with no material needed | heat from the Sun, a warm fire |

- **Good conductors** let heat pass easily: metals like copper, iron, aluminium. That's why pans are metal.
- **Poor conductors (insulators)** don't: wood, plastic, air, cloth. That's why pan handles are plastic or wood.
- **Woollen clothes** trap air, and air is a poor conductor, so your body heat stays in. That's why they keep you warm.
- **Dark colours absorb** more heat than light colours, so we wear light clothes in summer.

## Memory tips

- **"Clinical = Close to the body."** Short range, 35 to 42, with a kink.
- **"Kink = keep."** The kink keeps the reading.
- **37 is the magic number** for body temperature. Say "thirty-seven, healthy heaven".
- **Conduction, Convection, Radiation = Touch, Flow, Rays.** Touch (solids), Flow (liquids and gases), Rays (the Sun).
- **For conversions:** ×9/5 and +32 to go to Fahrenheit. Reverse it (−32, then ×5/9) to come back.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying heat and temperature are the same thing.
- Using a clinical thermometer for hot water.
- Reading the thermometer from above or below. Keep your eye **level** with the mercury.
- Mixing up the order when converting: for °C to °F you multiply first, then add 32.

## Quick card

- Temperature = how hot or cold. Heat = energy that flows from hot to cold.
- Lab thermometer: −10 to 110 °C. Clinical: 35 to 42 °C, has a kink. Body: 37 °C.
- °F = °C × 9/5 + 32. K = °C + 273.
- Conduction (solids), convection (liquids, gases), radiation (no material needed).
