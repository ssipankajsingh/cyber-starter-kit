## The big idea

Earth's land isn't flat. It has high places and low places, wet places and dry places. The shapes of the land are called **landforms**, and they decide a lot about how people live: what they grow, what they build, and what they do for a living.

## The main landforms

**Mountains**: very high, steep land, higher than the land around them. They're often cold and covered with forests or snow at the top. They are called:
- **Fold mountains**, made when land is pushed and folded: the **Himalayas** (young and still growing) and the **Alps**.
- **Block mountains**, made when a block of land is lifted between cracks.
- **Volcanic mountains**, made from lava and ash: **Mount Fuji** (Japan) and **Mount Kilimanjaro** (Africa).

India's **Aravalli** hills are among the oldest fold mountains, worn low by time. The **Western Ghats** run down India's west coast.

**Plateaus**: high, flat-topped land, like a table. The **Deccan Plateau** is in south India. The **Tibetan Plateau** is the highest in the world, often called the "roof of the world". Plateaus are rich in **minerals**, so there is a lot of mining.

**Plains**: wide, flat, low land. They're formed by rivers that carry soil down and spread it out (called **alluvium**). The **Indo-Gangetic Plain** in north India is one of the most fertile and most crowded places in the world.

**Valleys**: low land between hills or mountains, often with a river through it.

**Deserts**: very dry land with hardly any rain, and little plant life. The **Thar Desert** is in Rajasthan.

**Islands**: land surrounded by water on all sides. India's islands include the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** and **Lakshadweep**.

## How landforms shape life

| Landform | Farming | Other activities | People |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mountains** | little (steep slopes, cold), but terrace farming and orchards in some places | forestry, tourism, hydroelectricity from fast rivers | few people |
| **Plateaus** | some crops, often dry | mining (coal, iron, other minerals) | moderate |
| **Plains** | **excellent**, with fertile soil and water | cities, roads, railways, industry | **many people** |
| **Deserts** | very hard (needs irrigation) | animal rearing (camels), tourism | very few |
| **Coasts and islands** | fishing, coconut | ports, trade, tourism | depends |

Plains support the most people, because farming is easy, and transport and building are cheap. Mountains and deserts support the fewest, because they are hard to live in and farm.

## Memory tips

- **"Mountain is Mighty, Plateau is Plank, Plain is Pancake."** A mountain is tall and steep, a plateau is a high flat table, and a plain is flat and low.
- **Plains = people.** Both start with P, and most people live on plains.
- **Plateau = minerals.** Think "Plateau: Pits" (mines).
- **Himalayas are young and fold-type; Aravallis are old and worn.**
- **Match the landform to the job:** terraces on mountains, wheat on plains, camels in deserts, mines on plateaus.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Mixing up a plateau (high and flat) with a plain (low and flat).
- Saying the Himalayas are the oldest. They're among the **youngest** fold mountains. The Aravallis are among the oldest.
- Forgetting that landform + climate together shape life. A mountain and a desert are both hard to farm, for different reasons.
- Writing "Himalaya mountains are volcanic". They're fold mountains.

## Practice (do these on paper)

1. What is a landform?
2. Name the three types of mountains. Give one example of fold mountains.
3. What is a plateau? Name one in India.
4. How are plains formed? Why are they good for farming?
5. Why do mountains have few people?
6. Which landform is best for building cities, and why?
7. Name a desert in India.
8. Name two groups of islands that belong to India.
9. Why are plateaus important for industry?
10. How does a mountain help people (give two ways)?

## Quick card

- Mountains (fold, block, volcanic), plateaus, plains, valleys, deserts, islands
- Himalayas: young fold mountains. Deccan: plateau. Indo-Gangetic: fertile plain. Thar: desert.
- Plains: most people. Plateaus: minerals. Mountains and deserts: few people.
