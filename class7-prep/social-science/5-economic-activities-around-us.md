## The big idea

Everyone needs food, clothes and a home. Most of us also want a bicycle, a phone or a trip somewhere. To get these, people **work**: they grow, make, sell, teach, drive, treat people, and so on. Any work done to earn a living, or to make or provide things people need, is an **economic activity**.

**Needs and wants.** A **need** is something you can't do without (food, water, clothes, shelter). A **want** is something nice to have (a toy, a new phone). Economic activities exist to meet our needs first, and then our wants.

## Goods and services

- **Goods** are things you can touch: rice, shoes, a book.
- **Services** are work done for you: a teacher teaching, a doctor treating, a bus driver driving, a barber cutting hair.

People who make goods or provide services are **producers**. People who use them are **consumers**. Most of us are both.

## The three kinds of economic activity

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | getting things **directly from nature** | farming, fishing, mining, forestry, rearing animals, collecting honey |
| **Secondary** | **making** goods from raw materials | making cloth, steel, bread, furniture, building houses, factory work |
| **Tertiary** (service) | **services** that help the other two and everyone | teaching, medicine, shops, transport, banking, police, postal work |

**One thing, three jobs.** Think of a loaf of bread:
- A farmer grows wheat (**primary**).
- A mill grinds it into flour and a bakery bakes it (**secondary**).
- A shopkeeper sells it, and a truck delivers it (**tertiary**).

Each depends on the others. This is **interdependence**.

## Work in villages and cities

- **In villages**, most people do **primary** work: farming, animal rearing, fishing. Some are craftspeople (potters, weavers, blacksmiths).
- **In towns and cities**, most people do **secondary** and **tertiary** work: factories, offices, shops, schools, hospitals.

**Money and exchange.** Long ago, people swapped things directly (**barter**): wheat for cloth. That only works if each person wants what the other has. Money solved that. People are paid in money and use it to buy what they need.

## Memory tips

- **Take, Make, Serve.** Primary *takes* from nature, secondary *makes* things, tertiary *serves* people.
- **First, second, third in order.** Primary comes first because nature gives the raw material. Then secondary makes it. Then tertiary serves.
- **Ask one question:** "Is the person taking it from nature, making something from it, or serving someone?" Answer decides the type.
- **Needs before wants.** Roti and a roof before a remote-control car.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Putting a baker (secondary) or a shopkeeper (tertiary) under primary. Primary is only for taking from nature.
- Calling a teacher or doctor "secondary". They provide **services**, so they are tertiary.
- Forgetting that mining and fishing are primary too, not only farming.
- Mixing up goods and services.

## Quick card

- Needs first, then wants
- Goods (things) and services (work for others)
- Primary: from nature. Secondary: make things. Tertiary: services.
- Villages: mostly primary. Cities: mostly secondary and tertiary.
- Barter: swapping. Money: makes exchange easy.
