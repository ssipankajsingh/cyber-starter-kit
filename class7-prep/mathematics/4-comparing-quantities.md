## The big idea

How do you compare things? "Riya is 150 cm tall and her brother is 100 cm" is a start, but a **ratio** says it neatly: 150 : 100 is 3 : 2. And a **percentage** says "out of every 100". Shops use these every day, for discounts, profit and interest.

## Ratio

A ratio compares two amounts of the **same kind**, in the **same unit**. Write it as a : b, and simplify it like a fraction.

12 : 18 → divide both by 6 → **2 : 3**.

**Same unit first:** 40 cm to 1 m is 40 : 100 (change 1 m to 100 cm) = **2 : 5**.

## Percentage

"Per cent" means "out of 100". 25% = 25/100 = 1/4.

- **Percentage of a quantity:** 25% of 80 = 25/100 × 80 = **20**.
- **Fraction to percentage:** multiply by 100. 3/5 = 3/5 × 100 = **60%**.
- **"What percent is a of b?"** a/b × 100. 15 girls out of 40 students: 15/40 × 100 = **37.5%**.

## Profit and loss

- **CP** is what the shopkeeper paid. **SP** is what they sold it for.
- If **SP > CP**: **Profit = SP − CP**.
- If **CP > SP**: **Loss = CP − SP**.
- **Profit % = (Profit ÷ CP) × 100**, and **Loss % = (Loss ÷ CP) × 100**.

Profit and loss percentages are always worked out on the **cost price**, never the selling price.

## Simple interest

If you lend (or deposit) money, you earn extra, called **interest**. The money is the **principal (P)**, the interest rate is **R% per year**, and the time is **T years**.

**SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100**, and **Amount = P + SI**.

## Solved examples

**1. Simplify the ratio 12 : 18.**
Both divide by 6. Answer: **2 : 3**.

**2. A bag was bought for ₹250 and sold for ₹300. Find the profit %.**
Profit = 300 − 250 = ₹50. Profit % = 50 ÷ 250 × 100 = **20%**.

**3. A pen was bought for ₹400 and sold for ₹340. Find the loss %.**
Loss = 400 − 340 = ₹60. Loss % = 60 ÷ 400 × 100 = **15%**.

**4. Find the simple interest and amount on ₹2,000 at 5% per year for 3 years.**
SI = 2000 × 5 × 3 ÷ 100 = **₹300**. Amount = 2000 + 300 = **₹2,300**.

**5. A ₹500 shirt has a 10% discount. What do you pay?**
Discount = 10/100 × 500 = ₹50. You pay 500 − 50 = **₹450**.

## Memory tips

- **"CP is what I Paid. SP is what I Sold for."** (Cost Price, Selling Price.) Profit% and loss% always sit on top of CP: "Percent goes on CP."
- **"PRT over 100".** Say it as a chant: *Pee-Are-Tee by a hundred.* It's the only formula you need for simple interest.
- **Percent means "÷ 100".** So 40% is 40 ÷ 100 = 0.4. Say "forty per hundred" in your head.
- **Ratios are like recipes.** 2 : 3 means "2 cups of sugar for every 3 cups of flour". Same unit on both sides!
- **Unitary method:** if 8 pens cost ₹96, one pen costs 96 ÷ 8 = ₹12, so 5 pens cost 5 × 12 = **₹60**. Find the value of one, then multiply.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Ratio of 40 cm to 1 m is **not** 40 : 1. Make the units match first.
- Working out profit % on the selling price instead of the cost price.
- Forgetting to **add** the interest to the principal when the question asks for the **amount**.
- Writing 20 instead of 20% (or 0.2) for a percentage answer.
- Writing ratios the wrong way round: 3 boys to 5 girls is 3 : 5, not 5 : 3. The order matters.

## Formula card

- Percentage of a quantity = (percent ÷ 100) × quantity
- Profit = SP − CP, Loss = CP − SP
- Profit % = Profit ÷ CP × 100, Loss % = Loss ÷ CP × 100
- SI = P × R × T ÷ 100
- Amount = P + SI
