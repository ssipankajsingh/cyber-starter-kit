This is the biggest topic in the English paper (5 marks), so give it the most time.

## Tenses

A **tense** shows *when* something happens: now (present), before (past) or later (future). Each time has four forms, so there are 12 in all. Here they are for the verb **write**:

| | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | She writes. | She wrote. | She will write. |
| **Continuous** (happening, in progress) | She is writing. | She was writing. | She will be writing. |
| **Perfect** (finished, with a link to a time) | She has written. | She had written. | She will have written. |
| **Perfect continuous** (has been going on for a while) | She has been writing. | She had been writing. | She will have been writing. |

**What each one is for, with a clue to spot it**

| Tense | Use | Signal words | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple present | habits, facts | always, usually, every day | The sun **rises** in the east. He **plays** cricket every day. |
| Present continuous | happening right now | now, at the moment | I **am reading** a book. |
| Present perfect | done, with a result now | just, already, yet, ever, never | She **has finished** her homework. |
| Present perfect continuous | started earlier, still going | since, for | They **have been playing** since morning. |
| Simple past | finished in the past | yesterday, ago, last week | He **went** to school yesterday. |
| Past continuous | was in progress then | while, when (past) | I **was sleeping** when the phone rang. |
| Past perfect | happened before another past action | before, by the time | The train **had left** before we reached. |
| Past perfect continuous | had been going on before another past action | for, since (in the past) | She **had been waiting** for an hour when he came. |
| Simple future | will happen | tomorrow, next week | I **will call** you tomorrow. |
| Future continuous | will be in progress | this time tomorrow | This time tomorrow I **will be travelling**. |
| Future perfect | will be done by a time | by 5 p.m., by next year | By 5 p.m. I **will have finished**. |
| Future perfect continuous | will have been going on | by ... for | By June I **will have been living** here for five years. |

**Simple present tip:** with *he, she, it* (one person or thing), add **-s or -es** to the verb: *he plays, she goes, it rains.*

## Voices: active and passive

- **Active voice:** the subject **does** the action. *Riya writes a letter.*
- **Passive voice:** the subject **receives** the action. *A letter is written by Riya.*

**How to change active to passive (4 steps)**

1. Find the subject, the verb and the object. *(Riya / writes / a letter)*
2. Move the object to the front as the new subject. *(A letter ...)*
3. Use a form of **be** in the **same tense**, plus the **past participle** (the "-ed" or third form of the verb: written, eaten, built). *(... is written ...)*
4. Add **by** and the old subject (you can leave it out if it isn't important). *(... by Riya.)*

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | Mohan eats an apple. | An apple **is eaten** by Mohan. |
| Simple past | The farmer ploughed the field. | The field **was ploughed** by the farmer. |
| Simple future | They will build a bridge. | A bridge **will be built** by them. |
| Present continuous | Ravi is reading a book. | A book **is being read** by Ravi. |
| Past continuous | He was painting the wall. | The wall **was being painted** by him. |
| Present perfect | She has cooked dinner. | Dinner **has been cooked** by her. |
| Past perfect | The teacher had punished the boy. | The boy **had been punished** by the teacher. |

**Pronouns change too:** I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them. (*You* and *it* stay the same.) *She called me.* becomes *I was called by her.*

**Only sentences with an object can become passive.** *He sleeps* has no object, so there's nothing to move.

## Memory tips

- **Look for the signal word first.** *Yesterday* means simple past. *Since* means perfect. *Tomorrow* means future. That finds the tense in a second.
- **Present perfect = have/has + the third form.** *Has eaten, have gone.* If you see *have* or *has* before a verb ending in -en or -ed, it's usually perfect.
- **The passive always has "be" + the third form.** Check the be-verb matches the tense: *is/am/are* (present), *was/were* (past), *will be* (future).
- **The "by" test.** Add "by someone" to the end. If it still makes sense, it's passive.
- **Swap the sides.** Passive is a mirror of active. The object and the subject swap places.

## Mistakes to avoid

- *He go to school.* With he/she/it, add **-s**: **goes**.
- *I am knowing the answer.* Verbs like know, like, want and believe aren't normally used in continuous: **I know**.
- *A letter is wrote by her.* Passive needs the third form: **written**.
- Forgetting to change the pronoun: *The ball was hit by he.* Should be **by him**.
- Using the wrong be-verb: *A bridge is built by them* (present) is not the passive of *They will build a bridge*. That's **will be built**.

## Quick card

- Three times (past, present, future) x four forms (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
- Signal words: yesterday (past), now (continuous), since/for (perfect continuous), tomorrow (future)
- Passive = **be** (same tense) + **third form** (+ by ...)
- Change pronouns: I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them
