## The big idea

Most things around us are **mixtures**: two or more things mixed together. Rice has stones in it. Muddy water has soil in it. Sea water has salt in it.

We often need to **separate** them, to take out something we don't want (stones in rice) or to get something useful (salt from sea water). Which method we choose depends on what's in the mixture.

## The methods

| Method | What it does | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Handpicking** | pick out pieces by hand | stones from rice, bad grains from good |
| **Threshing** | beat or trample stalks to loosen the grain | getting wheat or rice grains out of harvested plants |
| **Winnowing** | let the wind blow away light husk while heavy grain falls down | separating husk from grain |
| **Sieving** | pass through a mesh; small pieces fall through, large stay | flour from bran, sand from gravel |
| **Sedimentation** | leave a mixture still; heavy particles settle at the bottom | sand or mud in water |
| **Decantation** | gently pour off the clear liquid on top, leaving the settled bit behind | after sedimentation |
| **Filtration** | pass the mixture through filter paper or cloth; solids stay behind | muddy water, tea leaves from tea |
| **Evaporation** | heat or sunlight turns the liquid to vapour and leaves the solid | salt from sea water |
| **Condensation** | cool a vapour to get liquid back | collecting water droplets from steam |
| **Magnetic separation** | a magnet pulls out iron | iron filings from sand |

**Evaporation plus condensation** together can give you pure water from salty water: boil it, catch the steam, cool it. This is called **distillation**.

## Mixing and dissolving

- If a solid mixes into a liquid completely and you can't see it, it **dissolves** (it's **soluble**). Salt and sugar dissolve in water.
- If it doesn't dissolve, it's **insoluble**. Sand and chalk don't.
- A **solution** is a liquid with something dissolved in it, like salt water.
- When no more can dissolve at that temperature, the solution is **saturated**. Heating the water lets it dissolve more.

## Choosing the right method

Ask two questions:

1. **Is the thing dissolved?** If yes, you need **evaporation** (to get the solid back). Filtration won't work, because dissolved salt passes right through the paper.
2. **If not dissolved, how big and how heavy are the pieces?** Big pieces: handpicking or sieving. Light against heavy: winnowing. Heavy particles in water: sedimentation, decantation, filtration.

**Example: cleaning muddy water**
1. Leave it still for a while (sedimentation). The mud settles.
2. Pour off the top (decantation).
3. Pass it through a cloth or filter paper (filtration) for the finest bits.

**Example: getting salt from sea water.** Spread it in shallow pans in the sun. The water evaporates and the salt is left. (This is how salt is made on the coast.)

## Memory tips

- **"Sieve for size, winnow for weight."** Sieving separates by size, winnowing by weight.
- **"Settle, Pour, Pass."** Sedimentation (settle), Decantation (pour), Filtration (pass). That's the muddy-water order.
- **"Dissolved? Evaporate."** If it's dissolved, filtering won't help.
- **"Eva-Pore-ation" and "Con-Dense-ation".** Evaporation: water goes up and away. Condensation: vapour *condenses* to drops, like on a cold glass.
- **Match the method to the mixture,** just like the tool to the job.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Trying to filter salt out of salt water. It's dissolved and goes straight through.
- Mixing up threshing (getting grain off the stalk) and winnowing (blowing away the husk).
- Saying decantation separates solids by itself. It only pours off the liquid after the solid has settled.
- Forgetting that sedimentation comes **before** decantation.

## Quick card

- Handpicking, threshing, winnowing, sieving: for solid mixtures
- Sedimentation, decantation, filtration: for solids in a liquid
- Evaporation: dissolved solid from liquid. Condensation: vapour back to liquid.
- Magnet: iron from other things
- Dissolved? Use evaporation. Not dissolved? Use filtration.
