## Articles

There are only three articles: **a**, **an** and **the**. They go in front of a noun.

**a / an** mean "one of many". You haven't pointed at a particular one.

- **a** goes before a *consonant sound*: a book, a dog, a university (it starts with a "yoo" sound), a one-rupee coin (it starts with a "w" sound).
- **an** goes before a *vowel sound*: an apple, an umbrella, an hour (the h is silent), an honest man.

It's about the **sound**, not the spelling. Say the word out loud and listen to the first sound.

**the** means "this particular one that we both know about".

Use **the**:
- for something already mentioned: *I saw a dog. **The** dog was big.*
- for one-of-a-kind things: the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky
- for rivers, oceans and mountain ranges: the Ganga, the Pacific, the Himalayas
- for the best, the biggest and other superlatives: the tallest boy
- for musical instruments: She plays **the** guitar.

**Don't** use an article:
- before names of people and most countries: Riya, India
- before languages, sports and subjects: I speak Hindi. He plays cricket. She likes maths.
- before meals: Let's have lunch.
- before general plurals and uncountable things: Dogs are loyal. Water is precious.

## Determiners

A **determiner** is a word that goes in front of a noun to tell you *which one* or *how many*. Articles are determiners. So are these:

| Kind | Examples | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those | pointing (this/these = near, that/those = far) |
| Possessive | my, your, his, her, its, our, their | who owns it |
| Number | one, two, first, second | exact amount or order |
| Quantity | some, any, many, much, few, little, all, several | how much or how many |
| Each-one words | each, every, either, neither | one at a time |
| Question words | which, what, whose | asking about a noun |

## Rules that get tested

- **many** goes with things you can count (many books). **much** goes with things you can't (much water).
- **few** means "hardly any" (a sad word). **a few** means "some" (a hopeful word). *She has few friends* is lonely. *She has a few friends* is fine. It's the same with **little** and **a little**, for uncountable things.
- **some** is for positive sentences and offers (*Would you like some tea?*). **any** is for negatives and questions (*I don't have any money. Do you have any pens?*).
- **each** and **every** are followed by a **singular** noun: *each boy*, *every girl*.
- **this** and **that** go with singular, **these** and **those** with plural: *this book, these books*.

## Memory tips

- **"Sound, not spelling."** *An hour*, *a university*. Say it aloud before you choose.
- **"The" means "you know the one I mean".** If the listener can't know which one you're talking about, use a/an.
- **"Many you can Count, much you can't."** Many books, much milk.
- **Few is Frowny, a few is Friendly.**

## Mistakes to avoid

- *an university*. Wrong. It's **a** university.
- *the India*, *the Riya*. Don't use "the" before names.
- *much books*. Books can be counted, so it's **many** books.
- *every boys*. Every takes a singular noun: **every boy**.

## Practice (do these on paper)

Fill in the blank with **a, an, the**, or **—** (no article), or with the right determiner.

1. She ate ___ apple. 
2. We waited for ___ hour.
3. He goes to ___ university in Delhi.
4. ___ sun rises in the east.
5. ___ Ganga is a holy river.
6. My sister plays ___ guitar.
7. We have ___ breakfast at 8 o'clock.
8. There isn't ___ milk left. (some / any)
9. How ___ books do you have? (many / much)
10. How ___ water is in the jug? (many / much)
11. She has ___ friends, so she often feels lonely. (few / a few)
12. ___ books on the shelf are mine. (This / These)
13. ___ student in the class got a prize. (Every / All)
14. Is there ___ pen on the desk? (a / an) The pen is black.

3. a (university starts with a "yoo" sound)
4. The (there is only one sun)
5. The (rivers take "the")
6. the (musical instrument)
7. — (no article before meals)
8. any (negative sentence)
9. many (books can be counted)
10. much (water can't be counted)
11. few (it means hardly any, which fits "lonely")
12. These (plural: books)
13. Every (followed by a singular noun)
14. a (the sound of "pen" is a consonant). Then "The pen" is correct, because we already mentioned it.

## Quick card

- a + consonant **sound**, an + vowel **sound**
- the = a particular one we both know
- many + countable, much + uncountable
- few = almost none, a few = some
- each / every + singular noun
