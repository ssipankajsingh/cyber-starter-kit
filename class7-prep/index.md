## Read this first

This is a free study aid for students preparing for the **Class VII admission exam** at Gurukul Kurukshetra. It follows the published syllabus (the marks table below), chapter by chapter.

It is **not** made by the school, and the school hasn't checked it. I haven't seen the school's past papers, so I don't know the exact style of the questions. Before you rely on any of it, ask the school three things: the exam date, the question format (and whether it's in English or Hindi), and the pass mark.

All the practice questions are written fresh for this site.

## The exam at a glance

This is straight from the syllabus. The total is 100 marks.

| Subject | Marks | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| English | 25 | Articles & Determiners (4), Nouns & Pronouns (4), Antonyms & Synonyms (4), Adjectives (4), Prepositions (4), Tenses & Voices (5) |
| Science | 25 | Temperature and its Measurement, Components of Food, Methods of Separation in Everyday Life, Motion and Measurement of Distance, Diversity in the Living World (5 each) |
| Mathematics | 25 | Exponents and Power, Area and Perimeter, Algebraic Expression, Comparing Quantities, Lines and Angles (5 each) |
| Social Science | 15 | Oceans and Continents, Land Forms and Life, Timeline and Sources of History, Grassroots Democracy Part 2 (Local Government in Rural Areas), Economic Activities Around Us (3 each) |
| Hindi | 10 | शब्द-विचार, पर्यायवाची शब्द, विलोम शब्द, अशुद्धि संशोधन, वाक्यांशों के लिए एक शब्द (2 each) |

Look at that table for a second. **English, Science and Maths are 75 of the 100 marks.** And inside each subject, every topic is worth the same. So skipping one Maths chapter throws away 5 marks, a fifth of the paper.

## How to use a chapter

1. **Read the notes** (about 15 minutes). Don't try to memorise yet.
2. **Read the memory tips.** They're the shortcuts that make it stick.
3. **Cover the solved examples** and try them yourself first.
4. **Do the practice questions on paper**, then check the answers.
5. **Take the quiz** the next day. A day later is better than straight away, because it shows what you really remember.

## A simple plan

There are 26 topics in total. If you have about four weeks:

- **Weeks 1 to 3:** around 9 topics a week, one or two a day. Spend 45 minutes on a new topic, then 10 minutes at the start of the next day going over yesterday's.
- **Week 4:** revise, redo the quizzes, and finish with one timed sitting where you work through a full subject without stopping.

Fewer weeks? Start with Maths, English and Science, then fit in the rest. Never skip a topic completely, even if you only read the notes and try three questions.

## Tips that help on the day

- **Do the questions you're sure of first.** Easy marks in the bank calm you down for the harder ones.
- **Give your time out by marks.** If a subject is 25 marks, don't spend half the time on one 2-mark question.
- **Read each question twice.** A lot of lost marks come from answering a slightly different question.
- **Show your working in Maths.** A wrong final answer with correct steps can still earn marks. A right answer with no steps sometimes doesn't.
- **Write the units.** 24 is not an answer if it should be 24 cm².
- **Check before you hand in.** Spend the last 5 minutes re-reading, not staring at the clock.

The night before:

- Pack pens, pencils, a ruler, an eraser and your admit card or whatever the school asks for.
- Skim your notes. Don't start anything new.
- Sleep. A tired brain forgets things you knew yesterday.

## Remembering things for longer

- **Revise after 1 day, 3 days and 7 days.** Seeing something again just as you're about to forget it is what makes it stick.
- **Test yourself instead of re-reading.** Close the page and write what you remember. It feels harder, but it works much better.
- **Teach it to someone.** Explain a chapter to a friend or a parent. Where you get stuck is where to study next.
- **Make a formula card.** One small card per subject, kept in your pocket. Read it while you wait for anything.
- **Silly rhymes and pictures work.** The sillier it is, the better you'll remember it.
