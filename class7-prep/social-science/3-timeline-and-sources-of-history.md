## The big idea

**History** is the study of the past: what happened, when, and why. Nobody was filming the past, so historians work like detectives. They use clues called **sources** to piece together what life was like.

## Timelines and dates

A **timeline** is a line that shows events in the order they happened, with dates.

**BCE and CE**
- **BCE** means "Before the Common Era". It counts **backwards**. The bigger the number, the longer ago: 500 BCE is longer ago than 200 BCE.
- **CE** means "Common Era" and counts **forwards** from year 1: 1947 CE, 2025 CE.
- (You may also see **BC** and **AD**. BC and BCE are the same, and AD and CE are the same.)
- The year 1 BCE is followed by 1 CE.

**Working out gaps**
- Both dates in CE (or both in BCE): subtract. From 1947 CE to 2025 CE is 78 years.
- One in BCE, one in CE: **add** them. From 500 BCE to 100 CE is about 500 + 100 = 600 years.
- Both in BCE, going forward in time: subtract the smaller from the larger. From 500 BCE to 200 BCE is 300 years.

A **century** is 100 years. A **decade** is 10 years. A **millennium** is 1000 years.

## Sources of history

Where do historians get their clues?

| Type | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Archaeological** | things left behind by people of the past, dug up or found | tools, pottery, coins, jewellery, buildings, ruins, bones |
| **Written (literary)** | things people wrote | books, letters, **manuscripts** (handwritten on palm leaf or birch bark), the Vedas |
| **Inscriptions** | writing carved on stone, metal or walls | Ashoka's edicts on rocks and pillars |
| **Oral** | stories, songs and sayings passed down by word of mouth | folk tales, ballads, family stories |

- **Archaeologists** dig up and study objects from the past.
- **Historians** study sources and decide what they tell us.
- **Primary sources** come from the time itself (a coin, a letter from that time). **Secondary sources** are written later about that time (a textbook, a book by a modern historian).

**Reading sources carefully.** A source can be biased. A king's inscription will say good things about the king. A historian asks: who made this, when, and why? They also try to check it against other sources.

## Early history in brief

- Long ago, people used tools made of stone. This long stretch of time is called the **Stone Age**.
- Early people hunted animals and gathered fruits and roots. Later they learned to farm and to live in villages.
- One of the earliest big cities in our part of the world was in the **Indus Valley**, about 4,500 years ago.

## Memory tips

- **BCE counts down to zero, CE counts up from zero.** Picture a number line with 0 in the middle.
- **"Both same side, subtract. Different sides, add."** That rule finds the gap between any two dates.
- **Four kinds of source: dig, write, carve, tell.** Dig up objects, written books, carved inscriptions, and stories told aloud.
- **Archaeologists dig, historians decide.** One finds the clues, the other works out what they mean.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Subtracting when the two dates are on different sides of year 1. You need to **add** them.
- Thinking a bigger BCE number means a later date. It means **earlier**.
- Confusing a primary and a secondary source. A coin from 300 BCE is primary. This page is secondary.
- Treating one source as the whole truth.
