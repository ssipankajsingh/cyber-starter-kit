## The big idea

Sometimes you don't know a number yet. In algebra, you use a **letter** to stand for it, like x or n, and then you write rules using that letter.

A square has 4 equal sides. If one side is *a*, the perimeter is 4 × *a*, written **4a**. That one little expression works for *every* square, big or small.

## The words

Take the expression **5x² − 3x + 7**.

- A **term** is one chunk, separated by + or −. This has 3 terms: 5x², −3x and 7.
- A **coefficient** is the number stuck to the letter. In −3x, the coefficient is **−3**. (Keep the sign.)
- A **constant** is a term with no letter: 7.
- A **variable** is the letter: x.

**By number of terms:**

| Terms | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | monomial | 7x |
| 2 | binomial | 3a + 2b |
| 3 | trinomial | x² + 3x − 1 |

Any expression with one or more terms is a **polynomial**.

## Like terms and unlike terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same letters with the same powers. Only the numbers in front can differ.

- 3xy and −2yx are like terms (same letters, same powers; the order doesn't matter).
- 3x and 3x² are **not** like terms (the powers differ).
- 5x and 5y are **not** like terms (different letters).

You can only add or subtract **like terms**. Think of them as the same sort of thing: 3 apples + 5 apples = 8 apples. But 3 apples + 5 bananas is just "3 apples and 5 bananas". It doesn't get any shorter.

## Adding and subtracting

**Add:** put like terms together and add their coefficients.
(2x + 3) + (4x − 5) = (2x + 4x) + (3 − 5) = **6x − 2**.

**Subtract:** *change every sign* of the second expression, then add.
(5a + 2b) − (3a − b) = 5a + 2b − 3a + b = **2a + 3b**.

(The minus outside the bracket flips both signs inside: −(3a) is −3a, and −(−b) is +b.)

## Finding the value

Swap the letter for the number, then work it out carefully.

Value of 3x − 2 when x = 4: 3 × 4 − 2 = 12 − 2 = **10**.
Value of x² + 2x when x = 3: 3² + 2 × 3 = 9 + 6 = **15**.

## Writing a statement as an expression

| In words | As an expression |
|---|---|
| 5 more than a number x | x + 5 |
| 5 less than a number x | x − 5 |
| twice a number y, minus 5 | 2y − 5 |
| 7 more than three times n | 3n + 7 |
| perimeter of a square of side a | 4a |

Watch "less than": 5 less than x is **x − 5**, not 5 − x.

## Solved examples

**1. Add (7x² + 3x) and (2x² − 5x).**
x² terms: 7x² + 2x² = 9x². x terms: 3x − 5x = −2x. Answer: **9x² − 2x**.

**2. Subtract (3m − 2n) from (5m + n).**
(5m + n) − (3m − 2n) = 5m + n − 3m + 2n = **2m + 3n**.
("Subtract A from B" means B − A. B comes first.)

**3. Find the value of 2a + 3b when a = 2 and b = 5.**
2 × 2 + 3 × 5 = 4 + 15 = **19**.

## Memory tips

- **"Like terms are twins."** Same letters, same powers. If they don't look identical (apart from the number in front), leave them alone.
- **"Subtract means flip."** Every sign in the second bracket flips. Write the flipped version on the next line so you don't forget.
- **Keep the sign with the number.** In 5x² − 3x, the coefficient of x is −3, not 3.
- **Check by trying a number.** To test your answer, put x = 1 (or any number) into both the question and your answer. If they give different results, something's wrong.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Adding unlike terms: 3x + 2y is **not** 5xy.
- Forgetting to flip signs when subtracting a bracket.
- Treating x² and 2x as the same. x² means x × x, while 2x means x + x.
- Reading "5 less than x" as 5 − x.

## Formula card

- Only add or subtract **like terms**.
- Subtracting a bracket: flip every sign inside, then add.
- Monomial = 1 term, binomial = 2, trinomial = 3.
- To find a value: swap in the number and work it out step by step.
